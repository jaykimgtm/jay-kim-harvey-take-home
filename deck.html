Part 1 · Intake

A successful hire, not a filled seat

A hire succeeds when the person hits the business goal and stays. So the intake runs in this order.

  1. 01PriorityWhat does Japan need most this year?
  2. 02SuccessWhat must be true at 6 and 12 months?
  3. 03ProfileWho can deliver that, and who can't?
  4. 04ProcessHow we assess, who decides, how fast.
  5. 05TimelineDates we can actually hit in Japan.

The test for every question

Does the answer change who I look for, where I look, or what I say to them? If not, it isn't an intake question.

Part 1 · Intake

What I ask the Head of Japan

Opening, both roles

“If only one of these can start by spring, which one, and why?”

Both are urgent, and I'm one recruiter. Making the choice together is the first partner move.

  1. Enterprise AE
  2. 1

    Which accounts will this person go after in year one, and what does a win look like at 12 months?

    Sets the target companies. People who sell to law firms and people who sell to corporate legal teams work at different companies.

  3. 2

    Let's look at 5–8 real profiles together. Who would you hire, and who wouldn't you?

    Hypothesis to test Harvey's Enterprise AEs elsewhere came from enterprise software (Salesforce, MongoDB, Rubrik, Wiz), not legal tech.

    Shows the real bar faster than a must-have list: language, background, seller or future lead.

  4. 3

    What level are we hiring at, and what can we offer?

    If the offer can't reach the people you describe, we find out now, not at offer stage.

  5. 4

    Who do you already know, and who is off-limits?

    Your network is the best first channel for a founding hire. Off-limits means customers and partners.

  6. 5

    How will we decide, and how fast?

    Interview steps, panel, final sign-off, and whether you take first calls with candidates yourself.

  1. Customer Success Manager
  2. 1

    Which accounts will they own, and what must they get done in the first 90 days?

    Shows how urgent this really is, and whether we need someone who knows law firms or corporate clients.

  3. 2

    Adoption and training, or commercial? Who owns renewals and expansion: this person, the AE or you?

    Each answer points to a different talent pool.

  4. 3

    How much does a legal background matter: required, a plus, or not needed?

    Decides whether I search former lawyers and legal knowledge staff, or software CSMs.

  5. 4

    What level and pay, and who's on the panel?

    Same logic as the AE: who is within reach, and how fast we can move.

Before the meeting Harvey's scorecard, interview steps and pay band from Recruiting Ops, and a short call with one Enterprise sales leader outside Japan. I walk in with a point of view, not a blank form.

Part 1 · Expectation setting

What I tell the Head of Japan upfront

Japan realities that change what “ASAP” means, each with what we agree on before I start.

Japan reality What it means for “ASAP” What we agree
Pool Small: about 50–150 reachable people, and 5–15 who have sold to Japanese legal buyers (my estimate). The AI labs are hiring the same profile. Headhunt by name. Job posts won't find them. Which requirement we relax first if the pool is thin.
Calendar Notice is usually a month, plus unused paid leave. Many firms pay bonuses only to people still employed on payment day (December, June–July). Offers in January mean starts in March–April. Target dates for each stage, instead of “ASAP”.
Pay Senior sellers compare offers on base salary and expect a lost bonus or stock to be covered. A US-style 50/50 plan reads as a pay cut. A Japan pay band and buy-out budget, approved before the first outreach.
Process Long processes lose Japanese candidates. The first casual meeting (カジュアル面談) should sell the role, not screen. Every extra round costs candidates. At most 4 rounds, feedback within 48 hours, you take first calls.
First hire Candidates will ask whether Harvey is staying in Japan, and who supports them. You are part of the pitch. A short story you tell every candidate, backed by the Japanese firms already on Harvey.
Agencies A main channel for senior hires in Japan, at about 35% of first-year pay. Useful, but they see the same small pool. Whether we use one, for which role, and the budget.

The partner deal, and the dates

I bring

  • 5–8 profiles to calibrate on in the first week
  • A weekly pipeline and market update
  • Problems raised early, with a proposed fix

I need from you

  • Feedback within 48 hours
  • Your time for first calls and the panel
  • Decisions on the dates we set together
  1. Days 1–2Prep: Harvey's data, calls with peer hiring managers
  2. Day 3Intake with the Head of Japan
  3. Week 1Calibrate on 5–8 real profiles
  4. Week 2Launch sourcing
  5. Mid–late JanOffers, after the December bonus is paid
  6. Mar–AprStarts
